Tax Planning Without the Panic: Building Better Systems for Business Owners

01 IN THIS EPISODE

Larry Hasson, tax partner at Ottawa accounting firm McIntyre & Associates and co-founder of the tax-payment fintech Remitian, walks David DiNardo through how he coaches new business owners to budget for sales tax, income tax and installments so they are never blindsided by a tax bill. He then explains how a nagging problem with his own clients (and his physician wife) missing CRA payments grew from a proof-of-concept tool inside his firm into a venture-backed startup now regulated in Canada and the US, led by MindBridge founder Solon Angel as CEO. The conversation covers controlled growth, when to incorporate, expanding into the States, and why Canadian founders tend to think too small.

02 KEY TAKEAWAYS
  1. Larry's rule for new self-employed clients: money that flows into the business account is not yours until HST, income tax and next year's installments are set aside; transfer a budgeted amount to personal and leave the rest behind.
  2. Year two is the cash crunch, because you pay last year's income tax, HST, and start quarterly installments for the current year all at once, which is why owners feel like their tax rate is 80 to 90 percent.
  3. Missing an installment is expensive money: CRA charged roughly 10 percent interest last year (about 8 percent now, Larry's number), it is not deductible, and a missed payroll remittance can trigger a 10 percent penalty in a single day.
  4. Incorporating is not automatically the right move; Larry builds a three-to-five-year plan first, and clients with personal goals like buying a house may stay unincorporated for years to use RRSP, TFSA and FHSA incentives.
  5. Remitian was validated in layers: first with Larry's wife as the only customer, then across McIntyre's clients, then with partner firms ZenBooks and Crowe Soberman, always charging for the tool to prove it was real.
  6. The startup only became a real business in late 2023 when Solon Angel pushed Larry to 'rightsize' the opportunity, raise capital, and target an enterprise firm rather than four-partner shops paying $15 to $20K a year.
  7. Larry's lightning-round advice: the one number to watch weekly is cash flow, the biggest CRA red flag is unreconciled HST and payroll, and the sticky note on his first laptop would say 'email less'.
03 SHOW NOTES

Larry Hasson is a CPA, CA and Director at McIntyre & Associates Professional Corporation in Ottawa, with a focus on tax planning, reorganizations, and business succession planning for clients including health care professionals.

In this episode, we talk about what strong tax strategy actually looks like when you zoom out beyond filing, and how great operators build systems that reduce mistakes, stress, and expensive surprises.

We also get into Larry’s builder side. He helped tackle a persistent pain point in accounting firms, getting clients to pay taxes correctly and on time, which led to building Remitian (formerly TaxPay), a platform designed to automate tax payments.

You will hear about:
- Why tax planning is really a systems problem, not an intelligence problem
- How business owners can think about structure, timing, and decision-making before it’s too late
- What it takes to modernize tax workflows and reduce penalties and friction
- Discipline, consistency, and performance habits (Larry also competes in Ironman triathlons)
