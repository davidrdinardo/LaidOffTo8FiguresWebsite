01 IN THIS EPISODE

Hugues, an Ottawa business lawyer who built his own boutique M&A firm, explains how he took a swim-school concept he first scouted in Australia and turned it into Kids Can Swim Canada, a chain of purpose-built, privately run learn-to-swim centres that opened six weeks before COVID and now runs three locations with about 175 team members. He walks through the economics of a $2.5M to $3.5M-per-site, 20-year-lease business, the break-even math that gave him the nerve to start, the free-trial funnel that converts at over 60 percent, and the metrics and corporate structure he uses to scale. The conversation is a case study in bringing a lawyer's discipline for systems and risk to a capital-intensive, community-driven service business.

02 KEY TAKEAWAYS
  1. Break the scary number down: Hugues needed 70 kids a day to break even, which is only 7 to 10 people an hour, and once he saw it that way the risk felt manageable.
  2. Remove every barrier to entry with a free trial and a guided tour, then track why people do or do not enroll; Kids Can Swim converts 62 to 67 percent of free trials into paying members.
  3. Systemize from day one, because two locations can be run by an owner filling gaps but three or more will fail without operating manuals and daily checklists, and a systemized, owner-independent business is what buyers pay a higher multiple for.
  4. Swim schools trade at roughly 3 to 4 times EBITDA with under four locations, 5 to 6 times with four to ten, and 6 to 10 times above ten, so scaling the same system multiplies enterprise value.
  5. Watch churn rate and wage ratio, not just enrollment, and adjust staffing to the seasonal cycle, such as running six lanes instead of nine in July and August to save about 30 percent in wages.
  6. Use one corporation per location under a holding company for creditor protection, cleaner per-location financials, and an easier sale down the road.
  7. Seek feedback from the customers who would buy your service, not from people who never took a risk, and ask for it every quarter even when it is uncomfortable to read.
