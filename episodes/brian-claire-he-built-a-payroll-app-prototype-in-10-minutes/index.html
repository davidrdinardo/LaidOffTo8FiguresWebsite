01 IN THIS EPISODE

Brian Claire, founder of Ottawa's Blueprint Accounting, explains how he turned a Slack conversation into a working payroll-app prototype in about ten minutes using Claude and Cursor, then validated it with a survey of 50 to 60 accounting firms before paying an agency to build the real product. He walks through the twelve-month path from prototype to paying clients, the rebrand from Payday to Nooma after a trademark conflict with ADP, co-founder and shareholder-agreement lessons, and why he thinks the speed of AI development lets small teams compete with incumbents like Wagepoint.

02 KEY TAKEAWAYS
  1. Brian built the first Payday prototype in about ten minutes: he had Claude summarize a Slack thread with his co-founder into a prompt, then handed that prompt to Cursor, and the only cost was token credits.
  2. A survey of roughly 50 to 60 accounting firms found they juggle five to six payroll platforms on average, which is why the multi-client dashboard (the Hub) got more interest than the payroll engine itself.
  3. The timeline from idea to paying clients was about six months: two months of prototype and conversations starting July 1, four months with an Australian agency working roughly 35 hours a week, and live by January.
  4. Payday was rebranded to Nooma after merging with developer Hugh's Nooma Track, partly because ADP owns a trademark on the word Payday.
  5. On partnerships Brian says to be aligned on vision, get your own lawyer rather than relying on the company's counsel, and understand exit terms like shotgun clauses and four-year vesting with a one-year cliff.
  6. Nooma's stated goal is to automate more than 80 percent of payroll prep with a human still reviewing every run; Brian frames it as letting one payroll specialist handle ten times the clients rather than replacing people.
  7. Brian's closing sticky note is 'discipline over motivation', and his standing advice for anyone learning AI is to tinker with the tools directly and build a reusable prompt library instead of only watching videos.
03 SHOW NOTES

Brian Claire got pushed into accounting by his mom, bounced through BlackBerry, the Ottawa Hospital and defence firms, then hung his shingle in 2018 and built Blueprint Accounting into a seven-person firm serving clients coast to coast. Along the way he kept running into the same problem: payroll tools built for one business at a time, not for an accounting firm running fifty of them.

So he summarized a Slack thread in Claude, pasted it into Cursor, and ten minutes later had a prototype. He showed it to accountant friends, surveyed 60 firms, found they were juggling five to six payroll platforms each, hired an agency to build it, launched to a room of 40 people with a live glitch, and signed his first paying client within six months. Then he merged with a developer who was building the same thing and rebranded to Nooma.

We get into why 300,000 accountants have left the industry, why the partnership model is dead, how a non-developer manages a dev agency, what to put in a shareholder agreement with co-founders, whether a payroll platform that replaces 80% of a person is still a $40-a-month product, and why Intuit might be coming for your clients.
