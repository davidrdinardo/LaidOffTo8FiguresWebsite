Getting Laid Off at 24 Was the Best Thing That Ever Happened to Me

WATCH ON YOUTUBE → LISTEN ON SPOTIFY →
01 IN THIS EPISODE

David DiNardo recounts being laid off from the Ottawa Hospital at 24, shortly after finishing his MBA, and why he considers it the best thing that ever happened to him. The layoff pushed him into entrepreneurship and a promise never to let someone else control his trajectory. He shares four lessons from building and selling his business: being vulnerable in your personal brand, the tax advantage of dividends over salary in Canada, building your company as a sellable asset with the exit in mind, and enduring years of pain before the hockey-stick reward.

02 KEY TAKEAWAYS
  1. Being laid off at 24 pushed David into entrepreneurship and a promise to never again let someone else have more control over his life than he did.
  2. Vulnerability makes a personal brand relatable; a podcast is an easy way to have deeper conversations and clip out the vulnerable moments.
  3. In Canada, a rising salary is eaten by higher tax brackets, while being incorporated lets you shelter money and decide how much to take out each year.
  4. Every day of building a business is an investment in yourself as the asset; a sound operational engine is what attracted multiple buyers when David sold after 10 years.
  5. Build with the exit in mind and know your industry's revenue multiple, since some businesses sell at one times revenue and others at two or even ten times.
  6. It took David seven years to match his hospital salary, after which he tripled and then quadrupled it; entrepreneurship is a long slow grind before the hockey stick.
