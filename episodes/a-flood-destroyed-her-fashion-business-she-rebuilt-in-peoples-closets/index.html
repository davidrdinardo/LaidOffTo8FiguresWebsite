01 IN THIS EPISODE

Rebecca Rowe, an Ottawa personal stylist trained at Pratt in New York and in Paris, spent eight years building a ready-to-wear brand for women with athletic body shapes before a basement flood destroyed her fall stock, spring samples, and patterns in a single August. Former customers started asking her to work in their closets, and she turned that into a styling business built on closet edits, personal shopping, and a photo style guide clients keep after she leaves. She and David cover how she diagnoses a shopping problem versus a styling problem, why she caps sessions at four hours, her anchor point method, the insurance lessons from the flood, and why every business needs some kind of online presence even if it is not social media.

02 KEY TAKEAWAYS
  1. Get product and equipment insurance and understand how your assets will be valued; Rebecca's custom patterns, years in the making, were paid out at the same $7.99 as store-bought ones.
  2. Almost every wardrobe complaint is either a shopping problem (a missing section: tops, bottoms, layers, or accessories) or a styling problem (too much and no way to combine it), and the fix is different for each.
  3. Cap a closet edit at about four hours, because decision fatigue sets in and people start giving away things they will call about the next day.
  4. Personal style is a formula: five levers (color, print, texture, details, shape) plus an aesthetic and a gender lean; push each lever until the client pushes back.
  5. One anchor point, a visually hard element at the neck, waist, or feet, makes an outfit feel complete and takes the blame for a bad outfit off the body and onto the clothes.
  6. Set two budgets before shopping, the overall number and the absolutely-not ceiling for a single item, and never push past a client's comfort or the purchase will sit unworn like everything else.
  7. Even if you hate social media, people are already talking about you online and AI is answering questions about you, so build a presence in whatever medium feels most authentic, whether audio, writing, photos, or video.
