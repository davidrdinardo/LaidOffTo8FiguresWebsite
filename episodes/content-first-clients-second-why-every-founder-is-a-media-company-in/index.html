Content First, Clients Second: Why Every Founder Is a Media Company in 2025.

01 IN THIS EPISODE

Three weeks into building Taxifi in public, David DiNardo reports the sobering result of launching the name, the why-story and early access across LinkedIn, Instagram and TikTok in one week: 15 signups after a year of daily posting. He argues that every founder now has to operate as a media company, because distribution, not the product, is the hard part, and lays out how he splits his day between creating and running the business.

02 KEY TAKEAWAYS
  1. A full week of launch content across three platforms produced 15 early-access signups after a year of posting daily, which David reads as proof that distribution is now the hard part of building a business.
  2. A creator David works with earned $0 in years one and two, $30,000 in year three, $75,000 in year four and $200,000 in year five, showing an audience takes years to compound before it pays.
  3. Each platform rewards different content: TikTok favours raw authenticity, Instagram feed posts need polish while Stories stay casual, and LinkedIn is a different audience again.
  4. Rather than spend $10,000 to $15,000 on a three-month radio ad, David paid a creator for a giveaway that drew 8,000 views, 1,000 comments and 200 new followers.
  5. Podcast guests who are creators show up nervous but unscripted and connect better with the audience than business owners who ask for questions in advance and pre-memorize answers.
  6. His working structure: mornings for creating content, afternoons for running the business, and no side ventures because founder-led storytelling cannot be outsourced.
  7. Taxifi's target user is the creator or solopreneur with a side gig who has no time for tax pain; the launch date is March 2, 2026 with founders rates for the early-access waitlist.
03 SHOW NOTES
