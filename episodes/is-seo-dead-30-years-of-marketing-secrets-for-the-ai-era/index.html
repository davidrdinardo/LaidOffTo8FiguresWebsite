01 IN THIS EPISODE

Stephen Lau, who won Yahoo's Website of the Week in 1996 and was David's first marketing call when he started his accounting firm in 2012, argues that SEO is not dead in the AI era but has changed shape: LLMs still draw their answers from published content, so a local business needs a far larger website that answers every question a customer could ask. He explains how his agency, rebranding from Salient Marketing to Salient AI, replaced its own project management and SEO software with in-house builds, and why it now runs short hourly experiments before committing clients to an AI project. The conversation widens into pressure on SaaS pricing, hiring for AI fluency, what students should do when junior jobs disappear, and Stephen's worry that most businesses and people are not adapting fast enough.

02 KEY TAKEAWAYS
  1. AI answers still come from content that exists online, so your website should be as large as possible, answering every question customers ask (including price) and comparing you to each competitor.
  2. Expect website traffic to drop while searches for your company name rise; if you are the source of the answer, people type in your name.
  3. Google and the LLMs rank local businesses on trust signals such as references on other sites, podcasts, reviews, and even pay-to-play award sites, so participate in them even when they seem dubious.
  4. Run small paid experiments by the hour before committing; Stephen's clients signed on after seeing their existing software replicated in about three hours.
  5. Just because AI can build something does not mean you should: weigh a $2,000 build against a $5-a-month subscription, but build your own when a vendor will not deliver the features or integrations your business needs.
  6. When hiring or vetting a vendor, ask which AI plan they use; someone on the $200 plan is practicing every day and someone on the free tier is not.
  7. Business owners do not have to do the AI work themselves, but they must see it in action and understand its impact so they can judge who to hire and whether they will survive the shift.
