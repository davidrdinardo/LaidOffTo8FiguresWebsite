01 IN THIS EPISODE

David DiNardo sits down with Alex Lawson, a carpenter who grew up relying on the food bank in Heron Gate, built Capital Framers into a 17-person company, and is now running for mayor of Ottawa. Lawson explains why he sees politics as an extension of entrepreneurship, how he writes policy by talking to bus drivers, police and outreach workers where policy meets reality, and what he would change on crime, drugs, the vacant unit tax, parking and transit. The conversation also covers how he removed himself as the bottleneck in his business, his abundance-over-scarcity leadership style, and what it is like to become a public figure overnight.

02 KEY TAKEAWAYS
  1. Alex Lawson never called himself an entrepreneur; carpentry, then the business, then the mayoral run were each just the next problem he decided he could do something about.
  2. Politics is communication, and business is a series of the right communications, so seek to understand first and remember the taxpayer is your employer, not your customer.
  3. Write policy by getting into the weeds with the people where policy meets reality: bus drivers for transit, police ride-alongs for crime, and street outreach workers for drugs and homelessness.
  4. Ottawa runs on leaking buckets; OC Transpo lost $54 million last year while providing poor service, and pouring more money in before patching the holes will not fix it.
  5. Ottawa has a minimum of 42 police officers on duty citywide, the same number as in 1995 before amalgamation, and Lawson wants to double that to 84 over a four-year term.
  6. Ottawa has six or seven places to get a clean pipe or needle but only two or three recovery beds with a week-long wait, so Lawson wants harm reduction paired with a real pathway to recovery.
  7. Lawson would scrap the vacant unit tax and slash paid parking, arguing that fees, tickets and garbage limits are picking taxpayers' pockets while core services decline.
  8. To stop being the bottleneck, give your team responsibility before they are fully ready and accept lower profit at first, because people have to build the muscle and find their own leadership style.
  9. Alex's sticky note on his first laptop would say: keep going.
