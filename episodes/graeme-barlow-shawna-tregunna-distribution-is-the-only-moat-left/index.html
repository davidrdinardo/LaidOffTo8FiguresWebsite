Graeme Barlow & Shawna Tregunna: Distribution Is the Only Moat Left

01 IN THIS EPISODE

Graeme Barlow and Shawna Tregunna, serial founders behind the FounderLink community and the new AI content product Human Strata, walk David DiNardo through how AI has flipped the economics of building a company. Their core argument is that software is now cheap to build, so distribution, personal brand, and the human layer around the tool are the only durable advantages left. The conversation gets tactical on pricing at whatever the market will bear, validating an idea with a landing page and meta ads before writing code, building a rejection muscle through cold calls, and why the gap between AI-first and legacy companies will only widen.

02 KEY TAKEAWAYS
  1. Building the product is no longer the moat; anyone with a cloud subscription can vibe code the same thing in 72 hours, so distribution and attention are where the competitive edge now lives.
  2. Sell the outcome, not the technology: putting 'AI-powered' on a website is like saying 'internet-powered', and prospects who barely use AI should never hear words like tokens, agents, or harnesses.
  3. Old cost-plus pricing is dead; price as high as the market will bear and let price-sensitive buyers go elsewhere, and aim at rich people's problems rather than margin-constrained small businesses.
  4. Wrap AI in a human layer (roughly 10/80/10 or 20/60/20, human at the front, AI in the middle, human checking the output) because customers will pay a premium for humans and the tech is becoming disposable.
  5. To validate an idea, build a landing page, run meta ads at 10 to 20 dollars a day until you have 500 to 1,000 emails and phone numbers, call those leads, and collect around 50,000 dollars in pre-sales before building anything.
  6. Treat feedback as data: ignore single comments, only act when themes repeat across people in your ideal client profile, and never take advice from someone who has not achieved what you want.
  7. Build a personal brand years before you need it, because it is the one asset that carries over between companies, and remove every barrier to creating content so it happens daily.
