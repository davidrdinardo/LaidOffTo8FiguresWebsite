Nobody Wants a Tax App: 5 Lessons From Building Taxifi in Public.

WATCH ON YOUTUBE → LISTEN ON SPOTIFY →
01 IN THIS EPISODE

David DiNardo reports from week three of building Taxifi in public, three months ahead of its March 2, 2026 launch, and shares the five lessons the first weeks taught him. He explains how EOS quarterly rocks keep him working on the business instead of in it, why he chose creators and sole proprietors as a single vertical, and why an app that does not deliver an outcome will not sell no matter how good the content is.

02 KEY TAKEAWAYS
  1. David runs Taxifi on EOS: four to six quarterly rocks per person, anything new goes on the bench for next quarter, and he completed all six Q4 2025 rocks, including launching the website, early access and the corporate structure.
  2. Taxifi's stated vision is a $100 million business valuation within five years, and every website, development and marketing task is worked backwards from that and from the March 2, 2026 launch date.
  3. Niche clarity beats serving everyone: David deliberately speaks only to creators and solopreneurs, and the DMs saying 'you're speaking to me' confirmed that generalist messaging would not land.
  4. People buy outcomes, not apps; Taxifi starts with sole proprietorships because David can automate at least 95% of that process, while incorporations are much harder.
  5. Building in public forces better decisions and generates its own content; documenting the journey means you are never short of things to post.
  6. You cannot automate clarity: David did the problem-definition work in a creative session, not in ChatGPT, because a polished AI answer is not necessarily the right one.
  7. Emotions are data too; as AI outperforms both you and your competitors on the work, your nerves, story and level of vulnerability become the differentiator.
03 SHOW NOTES

This week’s Taxifi build update came with a few lessons I didn’t expect, and they’re changing how I think about the product.

Here are the 5 biggest takeaways so far:

1) Nobody wants a tax app. They want the problem gone.
2) Niche makes people lean in. The clearer the “who,” the stronger the pull.
3) Judgment free is a real feature. People don’t just need software, they need a safe place to start. (So many people apologize for being behind.)
4) Simple beats clever. The clearest promise wins:
“One-click, judgment-free tax filing so you never think about taxes again.”
5) Building in public changes how you ship. More feedback, more accountability, and faster decisions.

If you’re building a product, a brand, or anything customer-facing, this episode is a reminder that clarity, safety, and simplicity are the real differentiators.
