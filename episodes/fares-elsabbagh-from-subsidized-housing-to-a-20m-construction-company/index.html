01 IN THIS EPISODE

Fares Elsabbagh, founder of Ottawa General Contractors, tells David DiNardo how a kid from subsidized housing who hated being told what to do turned a $50,000 stake and a digital marketing landing page into a design-build construction company doing $20 million a year. He walks through the near-sale at the ten-year mark, the shift from chasing sales to designing experiences for staff, subcontractors and clients, and the pivot to growth by acquisition. The back half is a practical look at Ottawa's new zoning rules and the economics of building 8 to 12-unit multiplexes, plus why both men now see AI as the same kind of opportunity digital marketing was 16 years ago.

02 KEY TAKEAWAYS
  1. Sell first and build the back end after: Fares had never lifted a hammer when a construction landing page started generating leads, and the company did a million dollars in its first year by getting the sale and then figuring out delivery.
  2. At $10 million in revenue with staff, subcontractors and homeowners all unhappy, he turned down a roughly $1 million buyout and instead rebuilt the business around delivering an experience to all three groups, which cut turnover and lifted reviews and referrals.
  3. Growing through acquisition beat the startup route: the team wrote down the exact business profile they wanted, reached out to about 1,000 companies, took roughly 30 meetings, and used a former BDC consultant to quarterback the deal.
  4. Under Ottawa's new zoning, almost any lot can become a multi-unit; staying at 12 units or fewer avoids site plan control, which can add $50,000 to $100,000 and an extra year or two.
  5. A rough model for a 10-unit build on a $500,000 lot is about $2.5 million all-in, a finished value of $3 to $3.5 million, and a refinance that returns most of the investor's cash; a permit takes about six months and construction about a year.
  6. Fares's advice to young people is to master AI now the way he mastered digital marketing in 2010: build a simple tool like an AI receptionist, give it to a business owner for free, get feedback, and grow from there.
  7. His sticky-note rule for himself and his kids: losers look for excuses and winners look for opportunities.
