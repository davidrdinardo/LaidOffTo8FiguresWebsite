01 IN THIS EPISODE

Cybersecurity entrepreneur Sergey Poltev, who runs a managed IT, AI and cybersecurity firm and wrote Hack Proof: A Guide to Cybersecurity for Your Business, walks David DiNardo through how small and medium businesses should adopt AI without getting hacked. He lays out his Simplify, Secure, Scale method, explains shadow AI audits, AI usage policies, phishing simulations and the cyber-insurance fine print that gets claims denied, and describes the three-person flash team he says every company needs before rolling out AI. The conversation matters because he argues technical debt and unmanaged AI agents can wipe out a company's valuation, while human-in-the-loop controls and a written AI policy are cheap to put in place early.

02 KEY TAKEAWAYS
  1. Sergey's Three S model: Simplify your tech stack first, then Secure it, then Scale with AI, because every extra tool and device costs more to protect.
  2. Start with a shadow IT / shadow AI audit to learn which tools employees are actually uploading company data into, then write an AI usage policy that names the tools, the data each can touch, and who approves outputs before they go public.
  3. Sergey's numbers: three out of four cyberattacks are now AI related, a breach sits undetected for about 200 days on average, and unaddressed technical debt can knock 50 percent off a company's valuation in an acquisition.
  4. Cyber insurance only pays about 60 percent of the time, per Sergey, and paying a PCI non-compliance fee on your merchant statement can be used to void your coverage, so read the policy prerequisites and prove employee training.
  5. Run phishing simulations and cybersecurity awareness training (CAT); Sergey sees failure rates fall from roughly 50 percent to 10 percent over a year, and a 5 to 10 dollar per month password manager closes the most common hole.
  6. Before deploying AI, assemble a three-role flash team: an AI coder, a business process mapper and a data analyst who knows where the data lives, then upskill existing staff into those roles.
  7. Keep a human in the loop for anything an agent does that moves money or sends messages, and add a call-back verification step for payment requests, just as 2FA became normal for logins.
