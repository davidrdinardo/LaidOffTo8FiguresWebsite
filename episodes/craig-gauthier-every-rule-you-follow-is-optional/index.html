01 IN THIS EPISODE

Craig Gauthier, strategy advisor, international speaker and author of the #1 bestseller Rules Optional, has worked in 33 countries, taught for five years and walked out of a hospital job where colleagues were counting down to a pension. He and David DiNardo make the case that trust and attention are today's two scarcest resources, that $150K earned through your own corporation nets far more than $150K as an employee, and that as AI eats knowledge work, plumbers will out-earn lawyers while online gets cheap and real life gets expensive. Craig runs his Break, Build, Become framework on David's layoff story, David names sales as the one skill he had to learn, and the two land on why the authentic self is a myth.

02 KEY TAKEAWAYS
  1. Craig's view: the two scarcest resources in today's world are trust and attention, and doom-scrolling dopamine leaves the brain in permanent fight mode.
  2. David's view: because Canada's small-business tax rate is about 12% versus over 50% at the top personal bracket, someone earning $150,000 through their own corporation nets far more than someone earning $150,000 at a hospital or government job.
  3. Craig's view: like fleas conditioned by a jar lid, people keep following inherited rules such as working 9-to-5, when almost everything except gravity, death and basic biology is a rewritable rule.
  4. Craig's view: AI will replace much knowledge work, so plumbers will soon out-earn lawyers, the online world becomes commoditized and cheap, and real-life experiences become stupidly expensive.
  5. Rules Optional runs on Break, Build, Become, with the R-U-L-E-S framework (rewrite the rule, unlock identity, learn, evaluate your time, share) and the CODE curve of clueless, overwhelmed, deliberate, effortless.
  6. David says sales was the one skill he had to learn to go from corporate to entrepreneur, and switching his accounting firm from hourly billing to a flat monthly subscription doubled its valuation from a one-times to a two-times multiple.
  7. Craig's view: the search for a single authentic self is a myth; David's North Star is being aligned with himself daily rather than a destination 20 years out, and a North Star is allowed to change.
