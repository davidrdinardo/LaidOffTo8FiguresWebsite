How to Build a High Trust Specialty Practice: Systems, Standards, and Reputation

WATCH ON YOUTUBE → LISTEN ON SPOTIFY →
01 IN THIS EPISODE

Dr. Rob Clark, the orthopedic surgeon behind Almonte Veterinary Services near Ottawa, explains how he built a referral practice doing only knees, hips and fractures at roughly 40% of the going rate while staying independent as corporate buyers pay 10 to 25 times earnings for vet clinics. David DiNardo, whose own dog Rob operated on, digs into the high-volume, low-cost model, the multi-week hiring process behind a 25 to 30 person team, and why owning the building and carrying no acquisition debt is the real competitive advantage. Rob also traces the path from a Thunder Bay high-school co-op to buying into the clinic in 2017 and paying it off during COVID.

02 KEY TAKEAWAYS
  1. Rob's model is high volume and low cost: Almonte charges about $4,000 for a dog knee surgery while Ottawa quotes run $10,000 to $12,000, and the case load that volume generates is what let him reach mastery, not the front-end margin.
  2. Vet clinics were selling to corporate consolidators at 10 to 14 times earnings and up to 25 times during COVID (Rob's numbers), and he argues that once a practice goes corporate it never returns to independent, so the seller is giving up both community goodwill and the future of the profession.
  3. Owning the building, buying the practice in 2017 at a 3 to 4 multiple, and paying everything off during the COVID surge means no 20 to 25% margin is needed to service acquisition debt, which is what allows the pricing to stay low.
  4. Turnover is expensive because it breaks an efficient system: losing 25% efficiency costs a surgery's worth of revenue per day in perpetuity, so Rob pays staff well and never throws a new hire straight into sterile protocol.
  5. Hiring runs through a candidate pool, a phone interview and a working interview over several weeks, because culture fit matters more than pay and every toxic exit has made the remaining team happier and willing to work harder.
  6. Rob asks new employees to bring problems while they are small and does frequent informal check-ins, since every issue pushed under the carpet eventually becomes a big one.
  7. His mentor Chuck Bruce's rule is that the focus is never on good, always on better, which is why the practice adopted arthroscopy even though it takes longer under anesthesia and has a steeper learning curve.
  8. Post-op recovery is a team sport: Rob asks for more restriction than he needs because owners deliver about 60% of what he asks, and a good outcome looks like 75% leg use at two weeks and no visible difference at six months.
