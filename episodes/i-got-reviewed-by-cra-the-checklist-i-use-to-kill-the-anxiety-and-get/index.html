I Got Reviewed by CRA: The Checklist I Use to Kill the Anxiety and Get Through It

WATCH ON YOUTUBE → LISTEN ON SPOTIFY →
01 IN THIS EPISODE

David DiNardo walks through the CRA review of his own corporate return, triggered when the legal fees from selling his accounting firm pushed his professional-fees category from roughly $5,000 to $20,000 in a single year. He shares the step-by-step process he used to send the auditor every invoice and receipt within two days and get the expense approved without penalties or interest. The checklist is built for any incorporated Canadian business owner who wants to stop dreading a CRA letter.

02 KEY TAKEAWAYS
  1. A big year-over-year swing in a single expense category, like professional fees jumping 4x, is what prompts CRA to ask for documentation, so unusual variances are the first thing to have backed up.
  2. Compare every expense category against the prior year at year-end and pull the invoice and receipt for each large entry then, not three years later when a letter arrives.
  3. Call the auditor first and ask exactly what they want and in what format, since every auditor works differently; David had the package submitted online within two days of receiving the letter.
  4. Keep a dedicated folder, such as a Gmail label for professional fees, so invoices are searchable in minutes; his response took under an hour to assemble.
  5. Follow up after submitting to confirm the auditor has everything, then move on; David's review closed a couple of months later with everything approved and no impact on his day-to-day.
  6. Do the bookkeeping in QuickBooks Online, Xero or an equivalent file and review the finished year's financials with your accountant early in the following year, and if a receipt is missing, ask the supplier for a copy.
03 SHOW NOTES

Getting a CRA review letter can spike your stress fast. I know the feeling, and in today’s episode I walk through the exact steps I use to stay calm, get organized, and respond properly when CRA comes knocking.

This is my anxiety-reduction checklist:

1) Review your financials and compare them to prior years
2) Pull receipts for major items so you can support the numbers quickly
3) Read the review request carefully, then respond with what they asked for (no extra noise)
4) Speak with CRA if needed and confirm what they still need
5) Follow up until you have closure in writing
6) Do what CRA wants, then stay on top of it so it doesn’t snowball later

If you’re a business owner and you want a clear, calm process for handling CRA reviews without spiraling, this episode will help.
