The Conference Trick No One Does (It Got Me the Speaker's Number)

WATCH ON YOUTUBE → LISTEN ON SPOTIFY →

01 IN THIS EPISODE

David DiNardo walks through the trick he uses at every conference to turn a speaker into a future partner. At SaaS North he picked a speaker whose company he wanted to work with, was the first one in the room and the last one out, and used the Q&A to say his name, his company and an honest testimonial in front of about 50 people. The speaker remembered him when ten others were waiting, and David left with his number, an intro to his content manager and a relationship he plans to build for months before he ever pitches a partnership.

02 KEY TAKEAWAYS

  1. Before a conference, pick the speakers you want to partner with, then be the first one in the room and the last one out of their session.
  2. Use the Q&A to stand up and say your name, your business and one real insight. Most people stay quiet, and it puts your name in the speaker's mind.
  3. An honest public testimonial for the other person's product does more than a pitch. David's covered a year and a half as a customer and $0 spent on marketing.
  4. Ten people lined up to ask the speaker how to build content. David went last and asked how to partner, which is why he was remembered.
  5. Do not push the partnership on day one. Build the relationship for six months, nine months or a year, until both products are ready.
  6. Partnerships have a funnel: going to the conference is the top, the conversation afterwards is the middle, and the partnership itself is the bottom.
  7. A partner with 90,000 users is a distribution channel, which is why building your network is one of the biggest jobs a founder has.

03 SHOW NOTES

I use one trick at every conference and almost nobody else does it. Last week at SaaS North I picked a speaker whose company I wanted to partner with. I was the first one in the room and the last one out. When the Q&A opened I stood up, said my name and my company, and gave an honest testimonial about his product in front of about 50 people.

In this episode I break down what happened next: why he remembered me when ten people were lined up to talk to him, how I left with his number and an intro to his content manager, and why I am not pitching the partnership yet. Going to the conference is the top of the funnel, the conversation is the middle, and the partnership is the bottom. If you skip the first two, the pitch never works.
