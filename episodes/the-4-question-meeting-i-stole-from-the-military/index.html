The 4-Question Meeting I Stole From the Military

WATCH ON YOUTUBE → LISTEN ON SPOTIFY →
01 IN THIS EPISODE

David DiNardo walks through the after action review his seven-person Taxifi team ran at the end of September, a four-question debrief borrowed from the military. He explains how to set up the meeting, why a facilitator and a no-blame rule matter, and shares real examples from the review, from an AI categorization target that slipped to a client goal missed because a one-year-old company has none of the goodwill his previous firm took a decade to build.

02 KEY TAKEAWAYS
  1. An after action review is four questions in order: what was supposed to happen, what actually happened, why was there a difference, and what will we do differently going forward.
  2. Appoint one facilitator, keep the whole meeting to 60 to 90 minutes, and give each question 15 to 20 minutes before attention drops off.
  3. Spend roughly 40 percent of the meeting on the last question, because that is where the action items, accountability and next month's plan come from.
  4. The review only works once blame is off the table; the first few sessions are about building enough psychological safety that a bookkeeper, developer and HR lead will each say what they actually see.
  5. Taxifi's September review surfaced that its AI categorization was meant to hit an 80 to 90 percent probability score, and that the team never defined its ideal client as planned.
  6. On missed sales targets, David's diagnosis is goodwill: his previous business had ten years of referrals, Taxifi has one, so expectations for new clients had to be reset.
  7. Keep the group between 7 and 15 people, run it at the end of every month, and let the team design the fixes themselves rather than handing down a top-down plan.
