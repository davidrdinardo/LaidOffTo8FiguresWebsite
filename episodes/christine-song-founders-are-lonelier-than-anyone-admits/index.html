Christine Song: Founders Are Lonelier Than Anyone Admits

WATCH ON YOUTUBE → LISTEN ON SPOTIFY →
01 IN THIS EPISODE

Christine Song, a longtime startup HR leader turned founder of 5to9 Society, explains why she left a decade of being the right hand to venture-backed CEOs to build a vetted, confidential dinner community for founders and operating leaders across Canada. She describes the isolation founders hide behind conviction, the reference checks and Chatham House rules that make her tables safe, and the post-COVID collapse in socializing that makes peer community matter more than ever. The conversation also covers the chip-on-your-shoulder mindset, why executives who don't tell CEOs the truth are a problem, and how AI bots are eroding trust in outreach.

02 KEY TAKEAWAYS
  1. Christine says it takes roughly 10 years to build a successful business, so pick something you can enjoy doing for a decade or you will quit in months.
  2. 5to9 Society dinners are capped at 12 to 15 people in a private dining room under Chatham House rules, and operators are admitted only after a 15-minute reference call with a CEO they reported to.
  3. Founders rarely get the truth from their own executives because they can fire them; peers at a dinner table have nothing to lose and will tell you what to do.
  4. Christine cites research that the average North American has cut socializing and networking by up to 25% since COVID, which she links to founder loneliness and leaders using AI as a therapist.
  5. The 5 to 9 after work is where the highest performers invest in themselves; do not expect your manager to fund your growth, and reading books or listening to podcasts costs nothing.
  6. A healthy chip on your shoulder means doing something about what pisses you off instead of blaming the world, which is why Christine started her own business after never getting an exit.
  7. David posted on TikTok three times a day for a year and landed a direct line to an American investor by tagging her; Christine landed a billionaire as a surprise dinner guest with a single cold email.
  8. AI-written outreach and comments are destroying trust: when Christine asked an audience how they would feel learning a message or post came from a bot, every hand went down.
03 SHOW NOTES

Christine Song spent 20 years in HR, the last 10 as the right hand to startup founders, where every problem in the company became her problem. She worked every weekend, answered Slack in the Disney World line, watched three of her founders get removed by the boards that funded them, and never got the exit she was promised. So last year she burned the boats and launched 5to9 Society: a vetted, confidential dinner community for founders and operating leaders, now 60 members across Toronto, Montreal, Ottawa and Vancouver.

We talk about what founders actually say behind closed doors, why your executives won&#39;t tell you the truth (you can fire them), why she reference-checks every member with their former CEO, how posting the unglamorous side of HR on LinkedIn meant she never applied for a job again, and why she gives herself 10 years before judging whether this worked.
