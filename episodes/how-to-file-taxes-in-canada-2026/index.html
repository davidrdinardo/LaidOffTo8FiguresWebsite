How To File Taxes in Canada 2026

01 IN THIS EPISODE

Nadia La Russa, who has run tax firms since founding Signature Financial in Thunder Bay in 1997 and now serves about 8,000 clients across Thunder Bay and Ottawa's Hunt Club Tax with a team of nine, answers David DiNardo's rapid-fire questions on filing Canadian personal taxes for 2026. She covers the April 30 and June 15 deadlines, CRA authorization and My CRA account setup, common deductions like RRSPs, tuition, medical and the T2200 home office claim, why couples should file together, and how installments, capital gains and depreciation work on rental property. The practical rule for anyone with a side gig: set aside 30% of that income for CRA before April 30.

02 KEY TAKEAWAYS
  1. Everyone's tax payment is due April 30; only the filing deadline moves to June 15 for sole proprietors and their spouses, so a self-employed person who files late should still make an estimated installment payment by April 30 to limit the 7% annual interest CRA is charging in Q2 2026.
  2. Nadia's rule for side-gig income is to set aside or prepay 30% of it (closer to 35% on top of $150,000 of employment income), because unlike a paycheque no tax is withheld and it stacks on top of employment income at your marginal rate, which reaches 49% for high earners.
  3. If you owe more than $3,000 on April 30, CRA will send quarterly installment notices for the following year; skipping them is not penalized but installment interest applies, currently 7%.
  4. Every T-slip you receive is also filed with CRA, so log into My CRA and cross-check that the slips in hand match what is on file; a preparer needs either CRA authorization or your last notice of assessment to see the same view.
  5. Deductions worth gathering: RRSP contributions, tuition (transferable to a parent or grandparent), medical receipts above 3% of income, donations, investment management and accounting fees, and a T2200-backed home office claim covering a square-footage share of utilities, insurance, mortgage interest, property tax or rent.
  6. Married and common-law couples should file together so medical, donation and tuition credits and pension splitting land on the spouse where they save the most; filing one return early can leave those transfers on the table.
  7. Rental income is not a small business (return due April 30) and a sold rental triggers capital gains tax on half the gain; depreciating the building lowers annual profit but raises the eventual gain, so the choice depends on whether you plan to sell or leave it to your children.
  8. The $2,500 CPP death benefit does not go on the deceased person's final return; it is reported on the executor's own return or, if the estate keeps earning income, on a T3 trust return.
