Building a 25-Year Ottawa Business: Service, Reviews, and the ePowerd Future

01 IN THIS EPISODE

Masood Yarmand of Yarmand Tech, the Ottawa outdoor power equipment dealer his family started in an 8-foot-wide Westboro storefront after immigrating from Afghanistan, marks the company's 25th year by explaining how it survived COVID, tariffs and a shift to robotic and electric mowers. He and David DiNardo dig into knowing your numbers, treating trust as currency, keeping family and business separate, and why blue-collar customers need a patient advisor rather than a hard sell on new technology. David also shares the $97,000 loss from his own first year in business and what networking taught him.

02 KEY TAKEAWAYS
  1. Yarmand Tech did not begin as a power equipment dealer; the family said yes to whatever neighbours brought in, from lawnmowers to skateboard ramps, and followed the line that produced consistent revenue.
  2. COVID forced changes in five days that would otherwise have taken five years: with the doors closed, Yarmand Tech turned into a Canada-wide online parts distribution centre overnight.
  3. Tariffs hit indirectly through supply-chain costs like steel, so Masood keeps the latest manufacturer price lists loaded in the point of sale and gives commercial customers a heads-up before prices or supply change.
  4. Masood treats trust as the company's currency and uses roughly 1,500 reviews across Google, Facebook and the parts site as the team's scorecard for staying on track.
  5. The first robot mower launched in 1995 (Masood credits Husqvarna), and the last one to two years have brought commercial autonomous machines that let one person plus a robot replace a two-man mowing crew.
  6. Masood's rule for family business: 'business is business, family is family' — heated discussions at work never carried over to family gatherings, and his brother and brother-in-law remain partners.
  7. David lost $97,000 in his first year because he assumed an MBA and an office sign would bring clients; the next year he went to every networking event and the business turned around.
