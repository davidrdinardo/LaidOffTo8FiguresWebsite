01 IN THIS EPISODE

Emilie Wilcox, an Ottawa entrepreneur who owns four businesses and works as a professional coach, describes the 2024 burnout that left her unable to sit or stand for more than two minutes at a time, and how learning that her pain was neuroplastic rather than structural let her rebuild. She walks David through how the nervous system's fight-or-flight and rest-and-digest states work, why high achievers get stuck in the first one, and the daily practices she now uses to move between them. The two compare how entrepreneurs can grind when needed without sacrificing their health, and Emilie argues that perfectionism and procrastination are often safety mechanisms rather than personality traits.

02 KEY TAKEAWAYS
  1. Emilie's pain after her Labour Day 2024 collapse was neuroplastic: scans showed no structural damage, and her nervous system was producing pain to protect her, so she recovered through exposure therapy, sitting, driving and lifting for slightly longer each time.
  2. Emilie cites a Stanford estimate that about 48 percent of the population has symptoms better explained by the nervous system than by any structural issue, including IBS, chronic fatigue and anxiety; her own IBS disappeared once she learned to rest.
  3. A healthy nervous system moves in and out of sympathetic (fight or flight) and parasympathetic (rest and digest); the problem is not stress itself but never coming back down, which is what a 6:30 gym session, a 8-to-6 desk day and a second workout was doing to her.
  4. Emilie colour-codes her calendar by how draining each item will be so every day balances activating and restorative work, and she says the two biggest deals of her career came while she was still lying on her back because she finally had space to think.
  5. Her quick tools for getting back to parasympathetic: the physiological sigh (two inhales, a hold, a longer exhale), rubbing the neck or ears to stimulate the vagus nerve, daily yoga nidra, zero phone notifications and shutting down by six.
  6. Perfectionism and procrastination are adaptive until they are maladaptive; Emilie challenges founders to ask whether delivering at 80 or 90 percent would produce the same results and free up energy elsewhere.
  7. Both agree AI favours experienced staff who know what a good result looks like, which raises the question of how juniors will ever reach senior level; David says his AI-driven accounting firm has moved from full AI to human plus AI.
