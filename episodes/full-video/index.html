01 IN THIS EPISODE

David DiNardo records a solo episode in the week he launched Taxifi, the app he built to solve the tax pain for creators after 15 years running an accounting company. He explains where the name came from, why the product targets the T2125 business return by connecting to the user's bank and CRA account, and why an AI product has to solve 95 percent of the problem before customers will trust it over a human. He also shares the nerves of running a waitlist launch for the first time, with the app due to go live March 1, 2026.

02 KEY TAKEAWAYS
  1. Taxifi targets one specific problem: creators filing the T2125 business return, with AI generating total income and expenses from a bank connection and pulling slips from the CRA so no separate bookkeeper is needed.
  2. The name combines Tax and FI, the FI standing for finance, because David expects the product to expand from taxes into helping creators understand where their money is after it lands from Shopify, Stan Store and other platforms.
  3. David's rule from a recent conference: an AI product must solve at least 95 percent of the problem end to end or the customer will go back to a human tax preparer.
  4. Launch week ran on a schedule: the name and logo on Monday, early access waitlist on Tuesday, the first podcast episode on Wednesday, with more content Thursday and Friday.
  5. AI got David's branding about 60 percent of the way; he still had to hire a professional designer, though the upfront AI research meant the designer delivered in a week instead of months.
  6. Unlike a service business where you sell and then do the work, a product has to be designed for a specific person from A to Z, which is why he is using the waitlist for real-time feedback before the March 1, 2026 launch.
  7. David commits to documenting the journey weekly and in the open, on the theory that helping even one person build an app or business in Canada is worth the vulnerability.
