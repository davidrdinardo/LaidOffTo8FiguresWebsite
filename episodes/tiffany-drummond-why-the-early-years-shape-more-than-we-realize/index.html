01 IN THIS EPISODE

Tiffany Drummond co-founded ProCare, an Ottawa child care centre, at 22 with business partner Aaron and has run it for 15 years, now with 16 staff and a 200-family wait list. She tells David DiNardo how she and Aaron went three years without a paycheque, why they chose one location over five, and how they built parent trust; the second half is a practical masterclass for parents on toys, screens, mealtimes and child-led play.

02 KEY TAKEAWAYS
  1. Tiffany and Aaron took no paycheque for the first three years of ProCare, reinvesting everything while Tiffany nannied and cleaned houses between before- and after-school shifts.
  2. Her partnership rule: pick a partner with a different skill set, schedule a monthly check-in conversation, and keep zero secrets between you; she and Aaron have never had an argument.
  3. A former employee's fabricated complaint triggered a year-long College of ECEs investigation that was dismissed; it taught her to keep staff relationships professional rather than best-friend close.
  4. ProCare stays at one location because child care is ratio-based (1:5 toddlers, 1:8 preschool, 1:3 infants), so every extra centre multiplies daily staffing risk and stress.
  5. Fewer toys is better: cap a small playroom at about 20 large toys, rotate stock monthly, donate every few months, and label bins with pictures so kids can tidy independently.
  6. Screen time guidance: 30 minutes a day, an hour at most, and never right before bed; do your own scrolling in the car before pickup so the next 90 minutes are fully present.
  7. Kids should be outside a minimum of two hours a day, and play becomes child-led at around age one, with the parent's job shifting to setting up the environment rather than saying no.
