How Boho and Glow Turned Candles Into a Community

WATCH ON YOUTUBE → LISTEN ON SPOTIFY →
01 IN THIS EPISODE

Emily Toffolo, founder of Ottawa candle and home-fragrance brand Boho and Glow, tells David DiNardo how she built an entirely organic business that has grown every year from her basement while raising two boys and later returning to a corporate job. She spent five to six months researching and testing before launch, took 45 orders on day one, landed her first wholesale account within weeks, and now supplies roughly 40 to 45 wholesale shops across Canada with no employees and no cold calls. The conversation is a practical playbook on brand consistency, seasonal launch timing, and daily Instagram habits for product founders.

02 KEY TAKEAWAYS
  1. Emily's number-one rule is brand consistency: she has never changed her logo or fonts in almost six years, and the placement of text and logo on packaging stays identical season to season so shoppers recognize Boho and Glow instantly.
  2. Before launching she spent a solid five to six months testing waxes, wicks, jars and oils (guided by supplier charts, then verified herself) while building the brand in parallel, so the cost was mostly time rather than money.
  3. Launch day brought about 45 orders from an Instagram audience of family and friends, and her first wholesale client, Third Avenue Spa in the Glebe, came within a couple of weeks after she brought a candle to an esthetician appointment.
  4. Wholesale now drives a large share of the business with roughly 40 to 45 stockists across Canada, all inbound through Instagram, word of mouth and other shops; she has never made a cold call.
  5. Launch early: she releases the Christmas collection in the second-last week of October after two to three weeks of teasing, and designs each seasonal collection 10 to 12 months ahead while the previous season's sales data is fresh.
  6. Her content system is a 30-minute weekend planning session in her phone notes, then one reel, post or carousel every day around 8:45 a.m., plus three to five stories, all edited inside Instagram.
  7. She protects her process: competitors asking for suppliers and box printers get a polite no until she has a proper course or playbook, and after five years fully organic she has just started paid Meta and Google ads through a two-person agency with strong results.
03 SHOW NOTES

Emily Ann started Boho and Glow with a simple idea, create candles that feel like a moment of calm in a busy day. What began as small batches and late nights testing scents turned into a studio, a loyal customer base, and tens of thousands of people following every launch.

In this episode we talk about how she built trust online, why she stayed committed when orders were slow, and the decisions that helped her grow from kitchen experiments to a full home fragrance brand. If you care about building a product people truly love, this conversation is for you.
