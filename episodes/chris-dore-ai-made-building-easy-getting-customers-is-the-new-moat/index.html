01 IN THIS EPISODE

Chris Dore, an AI consultant, Algonquin College entrepreneurship professor and co-founder of the venture studio Rogue Ventures, returns to talk with David about what changes for entrepreneurs when vibe-coding tools like Lovable, Base44 and Claude make building a product nearly free. Both agree that the development step is being solved and that the durable advantages now sit in front-end customer research and go-to-market, and they compare the major LLMs, discuss AI agents, government guardrails, Canadian entrepreneurship rates and what to tell students entering a market where entry-level digital jobs are shrinking. David shares how he is spending 80 percent of his time on distribution for Taxifi while development takes 20 percent.

02 KEY TAKEAWAYS
  1. Building an MVP is now the easy part; talking to customers before you build and having a real go-to-market strategy is what most founders skip and what will make go-to-market skills increasingly valuable.
  2. Pick one AI tool and get good at it instead of subscribing to everything: Gemini if you live in Google Workspace, ChatGPT for personal use, Claude if you want to build prototypes or do deep research.
  3. Run at least three customer-acquisition channels consistently, such as networking, LinkedIn and cold calls, then shift spend toward whichever one is producing most of your customers without shutting the other two off.
  4. Saving time with AI is only the first measure; the companies that win are the ones that decide what their people do with the recovered hours.
  5. The biggest opportunities for AI-native startups are niche, legacy-industry pain points that Google and Microsoft will not bother to solve, such as tax filing for small businesses or an AI receptionist for the trades.
  6. One in ten Americans is an entrepreneur versus one in twenty Canadians, and Chris argues Canada needs to promote entrepreneurship and risk-taking if it wants economic sovereignty.
  7. With knowledge available to everyone, hire and train for doers: critical thinking, communication, sales skills and the ability to judge whether AI output is good enough are the differentiators.
