01 IN THIS EPISODE

Kyle Soutar, an Ottawa CMA and serial entrepreneur who runs the fractional CFO firm Soutar Capital and Consulting, walks David DiNardo through what a fractional CFO actually does for a business trying to grow from $1 million to $5 million or beyond. He lays out the order of operations for a founder: get the corporate structure right, systematize with written policies, niche the product, then scale on a quarterly goal cadence tied to life goals as much as business goals. Soutar also shares the morning routine, audiobook habit and 'impossible goal' thinking he uses to keep his own mindset growing across roughly 70 entities.

02 KEY TAKEAWAYS
  1. A fractional CFO gives a small or mid-sized business the strategic finance partner a large company pays $200,000 to $300,000 a year for, at a fraction of the cost, with Soutar's team meeting clients at least twice a month for tactical and financial reviews.
  2. Soutar's starting structure for a new business is an operating company owned by a holding company from day one; a family trust is expensive and can be plugged in later at the most strategic moment.
  3. Write down policies and procedures early, even on a napkin or in Apple Notes, so the next hire can be handed the playbook; without systems you cannot 5x or 10x.
  4. Employment and contractor agreements go stale, so David recommends updating them every year or two with a lawyer, and remember an employee must receive something of value to sign a new one.
  5. From Benjamin Hardy's The Science of Scaling, set an 'impossible goal': ask what it would take to hit your 10-year revenue target in five years, then three, then two, because the bigger goal changes the decisions you make today.
  6. David's own experience: buying a $500,000, five-employee business and a $1 million, fifteen-employee business took almost the same effort, so an extra 5 to 10 percent of energy doubled the result.
  7. Soutar checks every bank balance he controls, personal and business, every single day, and says any owner should be doing the same.
  8. Onboarding a client early on, Soutar's team found over $100,000 in funds and programs the owner did not know they could access, which is why he says most founders do not know what they do not know.
