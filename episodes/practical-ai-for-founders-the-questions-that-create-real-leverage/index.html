01 IN THIS EPISODE

Chris Dore, CEO of the Ottawa AI consultancy Scarlett Nova, founder of the venture studio Rogue Ventures and an Algonquin College entrepreneurship professor, gives David DiNardo a practical playbook for putting AI to work in a small business. He explains why large language models hallucinate, how a 'universal prompt enhancer' turns one-line requests into two-page prompts, what separates an automation from an agent, and how his assessment-roadmap-workshop process picks the first three quick wins. The two also dig into whether an AI-enabled business sells for a higher multiple, why 'SaaS is dead', and which tools a sub-$1M company should actually pay for.

02 KEY TAKEAWAYS
  1. Dore never fully trusts model output: ChatGPT once wrote him a conference bio claiming a PhD and a jiu-jitsu black belt he does not have, so a human stays in the loop on anything with real risk, especially financial or tax advice.
  2. Instead of paying for prompt-engineering courses, build a custom GPT that acts as a 'universal prompt enhancer' and turns a one-sentence request into a one- to two-page structured prompt before you start a new chat.
  3. An automation moves one thing from A to B; an agent makes decisions about which information to gather, like Dore's pre-meeting agent that pulls the other person's LinkedIn profile and recent email history 10 to 15 minutes before every call.
  4. Every email Dore receives gets a first draft written automatically by an agent, and he estimates fine-tuned blog and LinkedIn content is now about 95 percent usable versus 80/20 a year earlier.
  5. Dore's engagement flow is assessment, roadmap, then workshop, priced around $7,500 to $15,000 for a 15-person company, with pilots ranging from roughly $10,000 to $100,000; he pushes for three small feasible wins first because early wins build confidence.
  6. Ask how you would put your own company out of business if you were standing outside it; Dore runs this exercise in every workshop because AI has made that attack far easier for a competitor to execute.
  7. Dore's argument for AI-enabling a business before an exit: buyers pay for processes, customers, relationships and knowledge, and AI systemizes the knowledge and lowers key-person risk, though he is still gathering data on how much the multiple actually improves.
  8. For a company under five people or $1 million in revenue, Dore recommends one large language model subscription (Gemini, ChatGPT, Claude or Perplexity; Copilot is his least favourite), Gamma for proposals and presentations, and vibe-coding tools like Base44, Lovable and Bolt for MVPs, and he warns free ChatGPT trades your company data for usage.
