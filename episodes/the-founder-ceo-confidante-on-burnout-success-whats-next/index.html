01 IN THIS EPISODE

Sheri Chiprout, an Ottawa founder and CEO confidante who once ran a makerspace, explains how losing her mother suddenly reset her career toward supporting founders through burnout and the 'what's next' stage after success. She walks David DiNardo through her six-month one-on-one format, the alignment work she does instead of pure business coaching, the four founder types behind a quiz she is building, and why she thinks the loneliness epidemic is the next problem worth solving.

02 KEY TAKEAWAYS
  1. Sheri works in six-month containers: three 90-minute sessions a month plus one integration week off, homework after nearly every session, and voice-note access in between because 'all the magic happens in between'.
  2. She calls the achiever's relentless output the 'machine gun effect'; it never switches off, so aligned founders point it at things that fill them up while misaligned founders burn out.
  3. Her staged model of founder support, borrowed from a Fullscript talk: early stage needs a business coach, the next stage needs a therapist, and only after that does the impact or legacy work begin.
  4. She is building a quiz that sorts founders into four types (visionary, mission-driven, architect and analytical) because feeling seen is the first step before setting personal success metrics.
  5. A founder who sees ten steps ahead of the team should plant seeds and bring in operators rather than force the vision; contraction from outgrowing your environment often gets misread as the people being the problem.
  6. Sheri points to 18 to 34 year olds as the crux of the loneliness epidemic and is validating a startup to help people host decentralized micro-community events.
  7. David's view on speed: with AI tools the window to test an idea is tiny, and he is vibe-coding a separate MVP in Perplexity while Taxifi itself is built by developers.
