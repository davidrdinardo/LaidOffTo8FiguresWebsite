01 IN THIS EPISODE

James Nguyen, David's former banker at RBC and now co-founder and CEO of Ottawa quantum-security company Quantropi, explains how he raised more than $25 million US with no institutional VCs, most of it from angels who had watched him work for a decade. He walks through his framework for entrepreneurship, the call from serial entrepreneur Jeff York that pulled him out of banking, meeting his physicist co-founder, and the whiteboard session that put a banker's name on a quantum patent. The conversation closes on the gap between Canadian and US capital, why confidence comes from surviving do-or-die decisions, and his advice to get your family on board before you start.

02 KEY TAKEAWAYS
  1. Nobody drops millions of dollars on a phone call; the money James raised came from ten years in banking where people watched his thinking, work ethic and network.
  2. Quantropi raised a bit over $25 million US: roughly $5 million in non-dilutive government grants and $20 million plus from angels, with no institutional VCs.
  3. An entrepreneur recognizes a real problem, filters signal from noise, executes, and builds a team smarter than themselves; the company's biggest asset is time, not money.
  4. If you have no network, you only need one person: find the few people whose superpower is knowing everyone and invest in those relationships.
  5. With no technical or physics background, James locked himself in a room with his chief scientist and kept asking questions until a problem that could have killed the company was solved, and his name ended up on the patent.
  6. Quantum computing checks a million rooms at once where today's computers check one at a time; AI finds patterns, quantum breaks locks.
  7. James's view: Canadian investors write slower, smaller cheques at lower valuations than US investors, but he is building in Canada anyway.
  8. Confidence comes from making do-or-die decisions and not dying, or dying and getting back up; and make sure your loved ones are on board before you start.
