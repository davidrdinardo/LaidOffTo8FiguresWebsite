The Founder Marketing Workshop: The Questions That Clarified Taxifi (Build in Public)

WATCH ON YOUTUBE → LISTEN ON SPOTIFY →
01 IN THIS EPISODE

David DiNardo shares the founder marketing framework he used to clarify Taxifi before launch: five questions covering vision and brand essence, audience and segments, category and competition, value promise, and brand voice. He works through each one with HVAC, plumbing and his own tax-app examples to show how the answers change who you market to and how, and explains why the exercise took him a month and why every new business owner should do it before launching.

02 KEY TAKEAWAYS
  1. Start with the problem that made you angry enough to build something, then write down exactly who you are solving it for; a residential family, a condo and a commercial client are three different customers even for the same furnace.
  2. Test brand essence with two prompts: if the app disappeared tomorrow, what gap would people feel, and if the app were a person, what three adjectives describe it?
  3. Define the audience narrowly (neighbourhood, city or province; income band; new build or old construction) and pick the channels for that person; Taxifi's audience is solopreneurs and creators who want to make money and never think about taxes.
  4. Market the outcome, not the craft: a plumber or tax preparer should sell 'fix my problem and give me the bill', because the customer does not care how pipes are made or how the return is prepared.
  5. Name your competitors explicitly (for Taxifi: TurboTax, H&R Block, accountants, DIY, spreadsheets) so you can answer 'why are you different' before a prospect asks; David is baffled that most owners cannot list theirs.
  6. Write down a value promise (Taxifi's is 'never think about taxes again'; an HVAC company's might be a 25-year furnace warranty) and a brand voice, then make sure employees and suppliers express the same voice so referrals compound.
  7. Ask ChatGPT for the five to seven questions to answer before marketing a business; the sixth piece, the user journey and experience, is covered in the next episode.
03 SHOW NOTES

If you’re starting a business, you need to get clear on the story, the customer, and the promise. This episode is me doing that in real time for Taxifi.

I walk through the exact framework and questions I’m using to sharpen the brand and the marketing, and I think every founder should do the same:

-Vision and brand essence: why this exists, what problem I’m solving, and what people should say after using it
-Audience: who we’re building for first (creators and sole proprietors), and what actually stresses them out about taxes
-Category and competition: what people love, what they hate, and what we can say that others can’t
-Value prop: the result users want and the simplest promise that lands
-Voice: how Taxifi should sound, and how it should make people feel

A few key takeaways from the exercise:
Taxifi needs to feel solid, caring, and non judgmental. People do not just need software, they need a safe place to start. And the promise has to be simple: one click, judgment free tax filing so you never think about taxes again.
