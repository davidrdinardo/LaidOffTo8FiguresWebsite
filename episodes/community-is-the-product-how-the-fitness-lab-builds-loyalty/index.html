01 IN THIS EPISODE

Adam Bracken, founder of The Fitness Lab in Ottawa, traces the studio from a franchise buy-in at 21 to four locations, a Lululemon merch partnership and a plan for 100 locations and $100 million in revenue across North America within ten years. He explains how giving away daily Instagram Live workouts for free during the pandemic, suspending every membership and topping up staff pay grew the community while competing gyms closed. David and Adam dig into the owner-operator equity model, strength-first programming and why community, not the workout, is the product.

02 KEY TAKEAWAYS
  1. Adam bought into a gym franchise at 21, sold his shares at 25 and opened The Fitness Lab in Manotick at 26, taking a five-kilometre, five-year non-compete just to get out; he says he earned about $9.72 an hour for the first few years.
  2. When his co-founder backed out the week the lease was due, Adam knocked on his cousin's door at 10 p.m. for the money rather than be the guy who announced a gym and never opened it.
  3. During the pandemic he ran free bodyweight workouts on Instagram Live seven days a week, growing from roughly 3,000 to 7,000 followers in a week and up to a thousand live participants, while suspending all memberships instead of charging for a makeshift online platform.
  4. Every shareholder owns a slice of all four locations rather than one, so owners willingly shift time and trainers to a struggling site like Kanata, which lost its lunch-hour tech crowd to remote work.
  5. Programming runs in three-week cycles with the same workout every Monday so members actually build strength, in contrast to the 'muscle confusion' circuit classes that dominated when he opened in 2016.
  6. Dropping 'Fitness' from the merch logo turned 'Lab' into a standalone lifestyle brand that non-members buy and wear, so customers pay to advertise the gym.
  7. The expansion plan, built with a business coach over a 12-month roadmap, is to find an 'Adam in Montreal' who recruits owner-operators city by city, targeting 100 locations and $100 million in revenue in 10 years.
