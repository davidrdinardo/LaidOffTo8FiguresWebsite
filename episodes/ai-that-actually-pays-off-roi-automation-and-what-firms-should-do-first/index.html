AI That Actually Pays Off: ROI, Automation, and What Firms Should Do First

01 IN THIS EPISODE

Julian White, founder of the Ottawa AI and automation consultancy Zentia AI, walks David DiNardo through a career that spans the dot-com bubble, a venture-backed mobile startup that stripped him of control, and twenty years of running a software services firm. He compares the JDS Uniphase crash to today's AI hype, explains how AI coding agents have collapsed months of work into hours, and describes the $995 automation audit he uses to find low-hanging fruit inside accounting and law firms. He also opens up about quitting alcohol after years as a high-functioning alcoholic and the balance-first routine that replaced it.

02 KEY TAKEAWAYS
  1. Julian's JDS Uniphase stock options were worth close to $10 million US on paper at the peak of the dot-com bubble, and he ultimately cashed out for about $1,500 after taking on roughly £150,000 of debt against the imagined windfall.
  2. Today's AI boom is a bubble that will likely see a stock-market correction, but unlike the dot-com era the underlying technology is real and is already replacing entry-level roles; Julian no longer hires interns and expects AI could put his own services company out of business in two to three years.
  3. A passion project Julian estimates would have taken a back-end and front-end developer three to four months a few years ago took him 20 hours with AI coding agents, versus roughly 60 hours a year earlier, because the agents now plan, test and fix their own work.
  4. When raising money, every investor signature can become leverage: one board member forced Julian to sign a unanimous-consent agreement as the price of his signature, which cost him control of the company and eventually pushed him out.
  5. Friends-and-family money is psychologically heavier than institutional money because those people know and trust you, and once you take outside capital even a cost-of-living raise for yourself needs board approval.
  6. Zentia's entry point is a $995 automation audit, a couple of calls and a written report that surfaces high-impact, low-cost workflows, and the first workflow Julian looks for in any firm is repetitive inbound email.
  7. Before rolling out AI tools, ask each team member whether they feel positive or threatened by AI; Julian finds it splits about 50/50, and unrealistic expectations about the learning period are the biggest early-adoption mistake.
  8. Julian's sticky-note advice for his younger self is 'find a mentor,' and his current rule is health and balance first: up at 3 a.m. for journaling and deep work, no email after 5 p.m., and never saying no when his son asks for a ride to the bus.
03 SHOW NOTES

AI is everywhere right now, but most professional services firms still struggle to turn it into real results.

In this episode, I sit down with Julian White, Founder and CEO of Zentia AI, where he helps accounting, legal, and engineering firms unlock ROI with AI and automation.

- How to think about AI as workflow leverage, not shiny tools
- Where firms usually get stuck (and how to avoid wasting time)
- What “AI readiness” really looks like before you automate anything
- How to find the highest ROI processes to improve first
- How leaders drive adoption, accountability, and real outcomes

If you run a service business, lead a team, or you are trying to modernize how work gets done, this conversation will give you a clear lens to start making smarter moves with AI.
